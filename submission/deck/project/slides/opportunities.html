Opportunities

An AI that designs the plan, and an engine that checks it.

How different is it?

Wind and microclimate tools are paid and built for architects. Tree-equity maps show where canopy is missing but do not design, cost or check a fix. EcoZone AI designs a costed mix of five interventions for a budget, checks each against ventilation and shade geometry, and explains it, on free data.

How will it solve the problem?

Ward and tree officers get a ranked, phased, costed plan for land the city already controls: footpaths, school yards, parking, medians and roofs, at the moment budgets are set. Planners see a large building’s effect before they decide on it.

USP of the solution

  1. Gemini designs; a deterministic engine scores; a greedy baseline runs alongside
  2. Shade measured as geometry where people wait
  3. Knows when trees backfire: no tree rows in deep, narrow lanes
  4. Every number traceable; planner input and AI text labelled

Existing tools either model wind for one building at a time or map where canopy is missing; none designs and costs a fix for a budget and checks it against ventilation. Our users are ward and tree officers who control footpaths, school yards, parking and roofs, and planners who review large buildings. The split is the point: Gemini designs, a deterministic engine scores, and a plain greedy baseline always runs alongside so we can see when the AI is not helping.
